I started writing these histories because I couldn't believe how many poor-quality software providers are out there. Some take advantage of owners who don't have the technical background to judge what they're being sold, and charge more for it.

By coincidence, I started working with an e-commerce shop hosted on AWS. Their previous provider had set up Magento 2 and then, after a commercial falling-out, stopped supporting it. A mutual friend put us in touch. The shop had three problems: the **bill**, the **backups**, and a **server that kept crashing**.

<Scrolly>
  <ArchDiagram slot="stage" diagram={backupDisk} title="The shop, as I found it" plate="Fig. 1.1" caption="Tap or hover a component for details." />

  <Step focus="users,ec2">
    The whole shop ran on one EC2 instance: Ubuntu, Apache, Magento. The instance type was a **c6i.2xlarge**, far larger than this workload needed. Even on the busiest days, Christmas included, CPU peaked at about 20%. The owners had been told the development wouldn't work on anything smaller.
  </Step>

  <Step focus="ec2,disk" show="disk">
    The site was down when I arrived. I opened SSH in the security group, restarted the server and did the Linux 101 round: `htop`, `df -h`, `systemctl status`, `journalctl -xe`. Magento's logs complained about failed writes, which pointed at the server rather than the CMS. And the disk was nearly full.
  </Step>

  <Step focus="cron,ec2,disk" show="cron">
    The disk filled up on a schedule, so I went looking for a scheduled task. The crontab had daily, weekly and monthly backups of the database and the site. Two jobs mattered: the **cleanup**, which never cleaned anything because of a typo, and the **S3 sync**.
  </Step>

  <Step focus="ec2,oldS3" show="provider,oldS3">
    The sync command pointed at a bucket the shop didn't own. Their backups were going to the old provider's AWS account.
  </Step>

  <Step focus="ec2,s3,iam" show="s3,iam">
    I created a bucket in the shop's own account, pointed the sync at it, and moved the schedule to weekly on Sundays and monthly on the first of the month. An **IAM role** attached to the instance allows it to put objects in that bucket, and nothing else.
  </Step>

  <Step focus="optimizer,ec2" show="optimizer">
    Then the bill. **Compute Optimizer** recommended an r6i.large. Magento's docs prefer compute-optimized instances, but the shop had custom code syncing Magento with their stock software, so I kept the memory: 16 GB. With backups taken and an Elastic IP keeping DNS stable, I stopped the instance, changed the type, and started it again.
  </Step>
</Scrolly>

## What I'd tell the owner of any shop in the cloud

- AWS is pay-per-use, and configuring it well is the customer's job. The right settings for *your* business are worth real money: here, 64% of the bill.
- If something in your AWS account looks suspicious, get a second opinion. There are a lot of unreliable cloud consultants. A starting point is checking whether a provider who claims to be an AWS partner is listed at [partners.amazonaws.com](https://partners.amazonaws.com/).

<Callout>
  Not every good provider is an AWS partner; it has its own costs and hoops. But if they *say* they are, checking takes a minute.
</Callout>
